Have you wondered how Google Maps shows live traffic? Let's look at the GPS data mechanism behind it in this video.

When you turn on location services on your phone and use Google Maps, GPS location and speed data goes from your mobile device to Google.

Google analyzes these anonymous GPS locations sent by many mobile users. By calculating the speed of users on a road, it creates a live traffic map.

To protect your privacy, Google uses data anonymization and aggregation. By combining data from many people in the same area, it makes it impossible to identify a single phone.

Also, Google permanently deletes the start and end points of every trip. Because of this, no one can find out where a vehicle came from or where it went.

If the minimum user threshold is not reached, a colored traffic overlay line will not be shown there.

Google Maps uses color-coded overlay lines to show traffic conditions on roads where live data is available.
